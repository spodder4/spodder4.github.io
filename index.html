How I think

Perspectives

Short reflections on engineering and architecture leadership.

Architecture leadership

Autonomy needs clear boundaries

Teams need room to make decisions close to their work. Enterprise alignment starts with clarity about which decisions stay local and which create consequences across teams.

Read the perspective

I favour shared principles, reusable patterns and explicit decision rights. Architecture review should concentrate on cross-domain dependencies and choices that are costly to reverse, while giving teams a clear path for routine decisions. The leadership challenge is to make those boundaries useful in daily engineering work.

AI & engineering

Automation changes work. Ownership still matters.

As AI agents take on more engineering tasks, leaders need to clarify who owns the result, how quality is checked and when a human decision is required.

Read the perspective

My starting point is the workflow: the agent's permitted actions, the evidence required before release and the owner accountable for the outcome. I would assess adoption through quality, flow and operational impact. More generated output alone would not demonstrate a better engineering system.

Engineering operating models

Measure the journey from idea to outcome

Engineering performance depends on the whole delivery system: priorities, hand-offs, dependencies, quality and operations.

Read the perspective

I look for where work waits and why. Better visibility across teams can expose constraints that local productivity measures miss. A useful leadership view combines delivery flow with quality, reliability and business outcomes, then uses that evidence to choose the next improvement.
